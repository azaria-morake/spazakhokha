The Township Paradox
02 / 13

R180 Billion in Annual Cash Trade, Yet Trapped by Friction

Across 150,000+ informal spaza shops and 10M+ daily shoppers, traditional card rails fail fractional micro-commerce.

1. Cash Friction & Crime

Physical cash is dangerous and expensive to maintain.

  • High armed robbery risk for spaza owners holding daily cash floats.
  • R15 - R35 ATM cash-out fees per commercial bank withdrawal.
  • Families lose 8% to 15% of disposable income just moving cash.
  • Constant coin shortages create counter disputes over sweet change.
2. The Micro-Payment Wall

Card interchange destroys township margins.

  • Card networks impose minimum swipe fees of R1.50 - R2.50.
  • A R5.00 bread slice or R3.00 snack loses 30% to 50% in fees.
  • Terminal hardware is costly and fragile in informal environments.
  • Merchants refuse card payments for high-frequency daily staples.
3. Paper Credit Tabs

Informal credit lacks transparency and auditing.

  • Neighborhood tabs ('makhaya credit') tracked in paper notebooks.
  • Zero audit trail, frequent customer disputes, 25%+ default rate.
  • No structured way for parents to limit student or child spending.
  • Bad debt threatens shop solvency and community trust.
To understand why digital payments have failed South African townships, look at unit economics. When a customer buys a R3 cold drink or R8 bread, card networks charge R1.50+ interchange. That wipes out the shopkeeper's entire profit margin. Meanwhile, shoppers pay high ATM fees just to withdraw wages in cash, only to walk into the township and risk being robbed. Spaza Uniti and UbuOne solve this paradox.
Field Research
03 / 13

Four Truths of Township Commerce

Field insights from Soweto, Tembisa, and Alexandra that directly dictated our engineering.

1. Fractional Purchasing

Observation: Shoppers buy single items throughout the day (R5 bread slice, R2 chappies, R3 soup) rather than bulk supermarket trips.

System Requirement: Unlimited sub-rand micro-transactions at R0 fee
2. One Inflow, Many Spends

Observation: Wages or SASSA social grants arrive once monthly; cash-out happens immediately to escape bank charges.

System Requirement: 1 Macro Open Payments inflow -> Spendable UBU Pool
3. Communal Family Budgets

Observation: Household heads budget daily allowances for children, student siblings, and grandparents who make daily purchases.

System Requirement: Family & Beneficiary RBAC with daily spending limits
4. Low Digital Literacy & Trust

Observation: Older shoppers and busy shopkeepers reject complex multi-factor banking apps and slow SMS OTPs.

System Requirement: Two-Tier Consent: Fast 4-Digit PINs or WhatsApp Links
Our field research uncovered that township finances are communal and fractional. A mother receives her salary once a month, but her household makes ten small purchases a day. If every purchase carried a network fee, the household would starve. Furthermore, mothers need to allocate money to their children at school without handing over cash that could be lost or stolen. These four insights directly inspired our architecture.

Security & Standards

RFC 9421 Signatures & GNAP Token Exchange

Full cryptographic compliance with the official Open Payments and Rafiki testnet specifications.

RFC 9421 HTTP Message Signatures

Every outgoing request to the Interledger testnet is cryptographically authenticated.

  • Algorithm: Ed25519 asymmetric key signing.
  • Covered Components: "@method", "@target-uri", "content-digest", and optional "authorization".
  • Content Digest: SHA-512 base64 digest formatted as sha-512=:<hash>:.
  • Zero Node Dependencies: Pure-Python implementation using cryptography.hazmat.
  • JWKS Verification: Key ID d644f013... verified live against testnet JWKS document.
GNAP Grant Protocol & ILP Packets

Fine-grained authorization for incoming and outgoing payments.

  • Interactive Top-Up: Generates consent URLs on auth.interledger-test.dev for user approval.
  • Non-Interactive Grants: Instant token acquisition for incoming payment creation.
  • Exact Debit Limits: Grants enforce strict debit amounts in specified asset code (EUR/ZAR).
  • Live ILP Streaming: Receipts include genuine ILP packet addresses (local.test.net...).
We implemented full cryptographic compliance with Open Payments standards. Our backend generates genuine RFC 9421 HTTP message signatures in pure Python using Ed25519 keys, matching the exact format required by Rafiki. We support GNAP for both interactive user consent and non-interactive server grants.
Engineering Breakthroughs
10 / 13

Challenges Encountered & How We Solved Them

Hard problems solved across protocol signing, unit economics, and distributed state.

1. Cloudflare 403 on Testnet

Challenge: auth.interledger-test.dev blocked Python's default urllib User-Agent with HTTP 403 Forbidden.

Resolution: Implemented RFC-compliant browser headers (SpazaUniti-OpenPayments/1.0) allowing clean API traversal.

2. RFC 9421 Signature Coverage

Challenge: Rafiki requires different covered components for auth server vs resource server (adding 'authorization').

Resolution: Engineered dynamic signing base generator supporting SHA-512 digests and exact GNAP token headers.

3. Micro-Payment Fee Destruction

Challenge: Traditional card rails charge R1.50+ swipe fees, making sub-R10 township purchases uneconomical.

Resolution: Built the 1:1 UBU local liquidity pool where 1 Open Payments inflow enables 100s of zero-fee micro-spends.

4. Real-Time Cross-App Sync

Challenge: Marketplace purchases on Spaza Uniti had to instantly reflect across UbuOne merchant cashiers' tills.

Resolution: Built unified SQLite transactional engine with row locking, synchronizing ubuone_ledger and customer balances.

Building on bleeding-edge payment protocols always brings challenges. We overcame Cloudflare WAF blocks on the testnet, solved exact RFC 9421 signature coverage requirements for Rafiki, engineered the 1:1 UBU buffer to defeat interchange fees, and unified the transactional ledger so purchases instantly reflect across all cashiers.
